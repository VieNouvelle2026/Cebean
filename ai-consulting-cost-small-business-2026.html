What Does AI Consulting Actually Cost for a Small Business?

Written by Pascale Décosse · Jun 23, 2026

Ask five AI consultants what a project costs and you'll get five different numbers, in five different formats. That's not because pricing is arbitrary — it's because "AI consulting" covers everything from a one-week readiness assessment to a six-figure custom system, and most quotes don't tell you which one you're actually being sold. Here's how the pricing actually breaks down in 2026, and how to tell if a number you've been quoted is reasonable.

The short answer

  • Readiness or discovery assessment: roughly $1,500–$25,000, fixed fee, typically one to a few weeks. Output is a written roadmap, not a build.
  • Single workflow automation: roughly $5,000–$15,000, fixed fee, for automating one well-defined process.
  • Full custom AI agent system: roughly $50,000–$150,000+, for a production system spanning a real workflow with integrations.
  • Hourly engagements: $150–$350/hr for independent consultants and boutique firms; $300–$600/hr and up for Big Four or MBB-tier firms.

Most small businesses land in the $10,000–$50,000 range for a first engagement — typically a readiness assessment paired with a pilot targeting one high-impact use case, not a company-wide rollout.

Why the range is so wide

Five things move the number more than anything else: what type of engagement it is (strategy-only versus strategy-plus-build), the seniority of who's actually doing the work, where they're based, how much compliance overhead your industry adds, and which of the three pricing models below you're being quoted under.

The three pricing models, compared

  • Hourly: maximum flexibility, but the risk sits with you — the total cost is open-ended, and the incentive structure rewards the consultant for taking longer. Makes sense for genuinely exploratory work where scope can't be defined upfront.
  • Fixed-fee / project-based: a defined price for a defined deliverable. Risk shifts to the consultant. Only works when the scope is actually well-defined — a fixed price on a vague deliverable is a red flag, not a benefit.
  • Retainer: a monthly hour-bank, usually for ongoing optimization after something is already live, not for the initial build.

The rule of thumb worth remembering: if a consultant can't quote you a fixed price for a discrete deliverable, it usually means either the scope isn't actually defined yet, or the pricing model is designed to favor them, not you.

Hidden costs to ask about upfront

The quoted consulting fee is rarely the full cost. Ask specifically about:

  • Third-party API usage (OpenAI, Anthropic, and similar model providers bill separately, and costs scale with usage)
  • Software or platform licensing (tools like Zapier or Make.com often carry their own monthly fees)
  • Post-launch monitoring and support — most consulting contracts end at go-live, and ongoing oversight is frequently a separate line item
  • Change management and training, so your team actually uses what got built

A realistic budget is the quoted fee plus roughly 20–40% for the items above — ask for these to be itemized before you sign, not after.

How to choose, based on what you actually need

  • You don't yet know where AI would actually help → start with a fixed-price discovery or readiness assessment, not a build contract.
  • You know exactly which workflow needs automating → a fixed-price project quote is appropriate, and you should expect one.
  • You need ongoing tuning after launch → a retainer makes sense here, not before.
  • Someone is quoting you a flat fee for a full agent system before doing any discovery → be cautious. That's usually a sign the scope was never actually defined, which tends to show up later as change orders.
