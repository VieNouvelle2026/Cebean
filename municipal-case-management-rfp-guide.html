What Municipal Case Management RFPs Actually Ask For

Written by Pascale Décosse · Jul 11, 2026

Municipalities across Canada are actively modernizing case management and workflow systems — intake portals, permitting, inspections, service requests — and posting the work through CanadaBuys federally or provincial portals like SEAO in Quebec. The global government case management software market was valued at over $8 billion in 2024 and is projected to more than double by 2034. But reading an actual municipal RFP tells you more about what evaluators want than any market size figure does.

A real example, not a hypothetical

The Town of Strathmore, Alberta issued an RFP for Process and System Modernization Services — a vendor-neutral consulting engagement, not tied to any specific software product. Its vendor questionnaire is a useful, concrete window into what these evaluations actually screen for:

  • Whether the bidder is vendor-neutral or vendor-agnostic, so recommendations aren't steered toward a specific product
  • Case studies specific to municipal process and system modernization, with measurable outcomes — not general private-sector examples
  • Demonstrated experience in change management, business requirements documentation (BRD), and system selection specifically
  • A clear, specific answer for how the firm defines and measures ROI on this type of project
  • How the proposed approach adapts to a municipality's limited budget and resource constraints
  • Named project delivery team resources with profiles and qualifications attached

None of that is about technology brand preference. It's entirely about whether the bidder actually understands municipal operations and can prove it with specifics, not general claims.

What this means if you're bidding

  • Generic private-sector case studies won't carry the same weight as municipal-specific examples, even anonymized or scoped ones.
  • Vendor-neutrality is often explicitly scored. A proposal that reads as a pitch for one specific software product, when the RFP asked for independent consulting, is working against itself.
  • Change management is not an afterthought in these evaluations — it's frequently a named, specific question, not folded into "project management" generally.
  • Named team members matter. Municipalities are evaluating who will actually show up to do the work, not just the firm's brand.

Where to actually find these opportunities

Federal opportunities post to CanadaBuys. Provincial and municipal opportunities are scattered — SEAO in Quebec, BC Bid in British Columbia, the Ontario Tenders Portal, and dozens of individual municipal procurement pages beyond those. There's no single feed that covers all of it, which is exactly why systematic monitoring across sources — not a one-time search — is what actually surfaces relevant opportunities before their deadlines.
