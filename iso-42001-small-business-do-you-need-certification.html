ISO 42001 for Small and Mid-Sized Businesses: Do You Need Certification? (2026)

Written by Pascale Décosse · Nov 6, 2026

Most small businesses asking about ISO/IEC 42001 are really asking two questions: is the standard useful to us, and do we need to pay an auditor to certify us? The answer to the first is usually yes. The answer to the second, for most businesses, is not yet.

What ISO/IEC 42001 is

Published in December 2023, ISO/IEC 42001 is the first international standard for an AI management system (AIMS): the policies, roles, processes, and evidence an organization uses to govern how it develops, provides, or uses AI.

  • Voluntary. No law requires it.
  • Any size. A 20-person firm using AI tools in client work is within scope.
  • A familiar structure. The requirements follow the same layout as ISO 27001 and ISO 9001, plus a catalogue of 38 controls you select from based on your own risks.

Alignment versus certification

This is the distinction that saves most businesses money.

  • Alignment. You build and run the management system and keep the evidence. No external audit.
  • Certification. An independent, accredited body audits your system and issues a three-year certificate, with annual check-ins.

A certificate is only as credible as the body behind it, so check accreditation (ANAB, UKAS, or the Standards Council of Canada). And keep the wording straight: "aligned with ISO/IEC 42001" and "ISO/IEC 42001 certified" are different claims. Use the second only once a certificate has been issued.

What it costs

Published figures vary widely, so we have left dollar amounts out. What matters is where the cost sits.

  • Audit fees scale with your size and scope. Get quotes from more than one accredited body.
  • Internal staff time is usually the larger cost: documentation, controls, training, and evidence.
  • Timeline. Usually months, and shorter if you already run ISO 27001 or ISO 9001.

How it compares

  • Use them together. The NIST AI RMF can serve as the risk method inside an ISO 42001 system.
  • A certificate is not EU AI Act compliance. ISO 42001 is not a harmonised standard under the Act, so it carries no presumption of conformity. It is still a useful foundation. EU timelines have been moving, so check current dates.

Do you need certification?

  • You use off-the-shelf AI tools internally and nobody is asking for proof → align, and skip certification for now. Start with an inventory of what's in use and a short acceptable use policy.
  • A customer, RFP, or insurer mentions ISO 42001 → ask exactly what they require. "Aligned with" and "certified to" are different requests, and the cost difference is large.
  • You sell AI-enabled products to enterprise or regulated buyers → align now and plan for certification, timed to your sales pipeline.
  • You are not sure where you stand → a readiness assessment answers that faster than guessing.

Who can certify you

Only an accredited certification body can issue the certificate. A consultant who helped build your system cannot certify it, and should not audit it either.

Quick answers

Is ISO 42001 certification mandatory?

No. It is a voluntary standard, and no law requires certification.

Can a small business get ISO 42001 certified?

Yes. The standard applies to organizations of any size, and you define the scope, so it can cover specific AI systems or business units.

Does ISO 42001 certification make us EU AI Act compliant?

No. It is not a harmonised standard under the Act, so a certificate does not create a presumption of conformity. It is a useful foundation for the governance the Act expects.
