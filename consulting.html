01 — Trade

You know you're overexposed. You just don't know where to start.

The ground under Canadian SMEs has been shifting fast, and uncertainty isn't a phase anymore — it's the new normal. Most businesses we talk to already know they're leaning on one market too heavily. What stops them isn't denial. It's not knowing where the actual risk sits, what order to tackle it in, or whether they're even ready to move yet.

That's the gap we close — for two different kinds of business at once. If you need a fast read on where you stand, you get that in minutes. If you're already committed to diversifying, you get the full roadmap: which markets are realistic now, which compliance gaps would trip you up, and which funding programs can carry the cost.

  • A readiness assessment that comes with actionable recommendations — not a generic checklist
  • A shortlist of potential markets specific to your industry and risk tolerance
  • A 12–24 month roadmap builder based on the recommendations you decide to accept
  • Direct routes to relevant resources based on your export readiness status — funding, EDC, FITT
  • A straight answer either way — if you're ready, a plan; if you're not yet, exactly what to fix first

This is also where our ExportReady tool came from — the same assessment, now self-serve. Read more on preparing to diversify when U.S. exports slow.

ProblemCanadian SMBs starting to think about international expansion had no self-serve way to begin. A proper current-state assessment meant hiring a consultant — and lean teams, already stretched thin navigating unfamiliar territory, didn't have the time or budget to make that the first step.
ApproachRather than build another consulting package to run the same assessment manually for every client, we built the assessment itself into software — so any SMB could get an initial export-readiness score on their own, without waiting for an engagement to start.
OutcomeExportReady evaluates a business across 9 readiness pillars, explains the rationale behind each score and why it matters, and sequences recommendations in the right order — so an SMB can start planning today instead of waiting weeks for a consultant's first look.
"As a small business owner exploring export for the first time, I don't have time to navigate complex processes. ExportReady was quick, easy to use, and immediately gave me a score with explanation highlighting the gaps in our strategy."Sophie D., Owner, Ontario SME
02 — AI

Adopt AI without the expensive false start

What you get: a rollout your staff actually use, tools chosen for your workflows instead of the loudest demo, and none of the compliance exposure that comes from moving fast without governance.

Most failed AI initiatives don't fail on technology — they fail on adoption, because nobody built the business case or prepared the people before the tool showed up. We start with the case, then the tool, then the training, in that order, at a pace your organization can actually absorb.

  • A readiness and risk assessment before you commit budget
  • A business case tied to a specific problem, not a general "AI strategy"
  • Governance that satisfies your compliance and regulatory obligations
  • Change management so the rollout survives contact with your team
  • Structured testing to validate the solution's trust, reliability, and scalability before full rollout

Testing is one of our specific areas of expertise — identifying how to efficiently and rigorously test AI solutions before they carry real operational weight. We also work particularly often with higher education, retail, telecom, and transportation organizations navigating procurement and policy constraints.

ProblemChecking that migrated data moved over correctly isn't enough — you also need to know it keeps working right once real, everyday activity happens to it later. A past migration had missed exactly this: the data looked fine on day one, but nobody had checked what would happen months later when a normal transaction touched that same record in the new system.
ApproachHaving seen that gap cause a problem before, we recommended expanding the test plan to cover not just "did the data move correctly" but "does it still behave correctly when someone does something normal to it afterward" — using a customer renewal as the test case.
OutcomeThe gap was added to the test plan and caught before launch, instead of surfacing later as a real customer's renewal silently breaking.
"Good call, we should add this scenario."Technical Lead, Telecom

Get the plan across the finish line

What you get: a transformation shaped by every type of implementation we've run — SaaS, custom apps, enterprise systems, migrations, robotic process automation, websites — so risks and issues get caught early, not after they've become expensive.

We've done enough of these to know what actually works and what only looks good in a proposal. Case study

Advancing a Digital Experience Platform strategy across a fragmented institution

The situation: A higher education institution was managing dozens of decentralized websites, each with its own approach to content management — inconsistent user experience, fragmented branding, and analytics nobody had the access or knowledge to actually use.

The approach: A structured current-state assessment came first, built through stakeholder workshops that surfaced real disagreement about the problem itself. That informed an architecture decision — a hybrid CMS model balancing flexibility with the institution's actual internal skillset — and, critically, a Target Operating Model defining who would own what once the new platform existed.

The outcome: A prioritized, RFP-ready requirements set, stakeholder buy-in strong enough that people volunteered for vendor evaluations, and a multi-year roadmap the institution could actually execute against. The Target Operating Model work alone reshaped the org chart — new roles were created specifically for the future state, and a function was relocated into IT so it would actually be supported long-term instead of orphaned in a department without the technical capacity to maintain it.

"Technology alone cannot solve systemic fragmentation — true transformation begins with aligning people, purpose, and process before selecting the platform."

How we actually run this

research

Analyst & peer research

Independent research (including analyst firms) and direct consultations with peer organizations running comparable systems — real-world lessons, not just vendor claims.

assess

Current state, right-sized

Enough depth to be actionable, not so much it stalls momentum — including where change fatigue already exists among the people who'll live with the result.

procure

Strategic procurement

Requirements and evaluation criteria shaped by research, not guesswork — including lessons from a cancelled RFP, where unclear scope produced bids too different to fairly compare.

measure

KPIs that outlast go-live

Adoption, usage, and satisfaction tracked at 6, 12, and 18 months post-implementation — not just a launch-day success metric.

ProblemAn RFP evaluation process was set to review every bid in full, wasting evaluator time on vendors that weren't qualified from the start.
ApproachRestructured the evaluation into stages, filtering unqualified bids out at stage 1 before the full evaluation team spent time on them.
OutcomeEvaluators only reviewed bids that had already cleared a qualification bar — less wasted time, a faster process.
