AI Strategy & Implementation

Adopt AI without the expensive false start

What you get: a rollout your staff actually use, tools chosen for your workflows instead of the loudest demo, and none of the compliance exposure that comes from moving fast without governance.

Most failed AI initiatives don't fail on technology — they fail on adoption, because nobody built the business case or prepared the people before the tool showed up. We start with the case, then the tool, then the training, in that order, at a pace your organization can actually absorb.

  • A readiness and risk assessment before you commit budget
  • A business case tied to a specific problem, not a general "AI strategy"
  • Governance that satisfies your compliance and regulatory obligations
  • Change management so the rollout survives contact with your team
  • Structured testing to validate the solution's trust, reliability, and scalability before full rollout

Testing is one of our specific areas of expertise — identifying how to efficiently and rigorously test AI solutions before they carry real operational weight. We also work particularly often with higher education, retail, telecom, and transportation organizations navigating procurement and policy constraints.

Same automation, two branches — testing is the fork between trust lost and trust earned.

When an AI implementation or automation gives the wrong result even once, trust in it disappears immediately — and it's hard to win back. That first bad result doesn't just cost the project its ROI; it makes every future AI initiative harder to get buy-in for.

We test every scenario a system will actually encounter — including what happens well after go-live, like a normal transaction such as a renewal — before handing anything off to a downstream system or an AI agent to automate. Getting it right the first time isn't optional. It's the only way AI earns the trust it needs to deliver a return.

The gap was caught and fixed before launch — protecting the one thing an AI rollout can't recover once it's lost: the trust of the people using it.

Case study

Advancing a Digital Experience Platform strategy across a fragmented institution

The situation: A higher education institution was managing dozens of decentralized websites, each with its own approach to content management — inconsistent user experience, fragmented branding, and analytics nobody had the access or knowledge to actually use.

The approach: A structured current-state assessment came first, built through stakeholder workshops that surfaced real disagreement about the problem itself. That informed an architecture decision — a hybrid CMS model balancing flexibility with the institution's actual internal skillset — and, critically, a Target Operating Model defining who would own what once the new platform existed.

The outcome: A prioritized, RFP-ready requirements set, stakeholder buy-in strong enough that people volunteered for vendor evaluations, and a multi-year roadmap the institution could actually execute against. The Target Operating Model work alone reshaped the org chart — new roles were created specifically for the future state, and a function was relocated into IT so it would actually be supported long-term instead of orphaned in a department without the technical capacity to maintain it.

"Technology alone cannot solve systemic fragmentation — true transformation begins with aligning people, purpose, and process before selecting the platform."

KPIs that outlast go-live

Adoption, usage, and satisfaction tracked at 6, 12, and 18 months post-implementation — not just a launch-day success metric.

An RFP evaluation process was set to review every bid in full, wasting evaluator time on vendors that weren't qualified from the start.

Restructured the evaluation into stages, filtering unqualified bids out at stage 1 before the full evaluation team spent time on them.

Evaluators only reviewed bids that had already cleared a qualification bar — less wasted time, a faster process.

Trade & Export Risk

Start with ExportReady. Bring in consulting when you need more.

ExportReady gives you the assessment and the roadmap to start with. When you need an extra pair of hands to plan the expansion itself, or your situation goes beyond what the tool covers, direct funding applications, hands-on market entry, negotiation, that's consulting.

The ground under Canadian SMEs has been shifting fast, and uncertainty isn't a phase anymore, it's the new normal. Most businesses we talk to already know they're leaning on one market too heavily. What stops them isn't denial. It's not knowing where the actual risk sits, what order to tackle it in, or whether they're even ready to move yet.

ExportReady solves the first part on its own: a self-serve readiness assessment and a 12–24 month roadmap, available any time, without waiting for an engagement to start. Consulting picks up from there, for the parts a tool can't do by itself:

  • Direct funding applications: CanExport, EDC, FITT, and other programs
  • Hands-on market entry work: partnerships, distribution, on-the-ground research
  • Negotiation and relationship-building in a new market
  • Anything specific to your industry a general assessment can't cover
